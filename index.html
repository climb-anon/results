Full Motion Rollouts

Finals of the Boulder Japan Cup 2024, where many of the climbers rank in the world's top 20.

Each climb is trained and evaluated in segments to save compute, though nothing prevents running it whole. The motion can jump at segment boundaries, which are colored in orange on the video timeline. Overlapping segments by about a second would smooth them.

Bodies are drawn as their separate collision shapes, so hand and foot contacts stay visible through the gaps from any angle. Drawing a skinned body instead would be a simple swap.

Comparisons

Each climb runs in segments (marks on the timeline). A segment whose training reaches the 48 h limit is evaluated at its last checkpoint before the limit, marked “timed out”. If a segment completes training but still falls, it will be marked “fell”, in which case the frame is frozen until the next segment and starts again from that segment's first pose.

Residual Force Control (RFC) was trained on one segment per climb; switch to that segment to see all three methods. Unlike our method, RFC has collision and residual force on from the start; it has the same curriculum that drives the residual force to zero as our method.
